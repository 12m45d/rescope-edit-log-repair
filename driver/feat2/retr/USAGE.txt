A. CONTENT WORDS AND THE LOOKUP RULE (fixed in advance; code: stoplist.py, retr.lookup_decide)
  token         a maximal run of Unicode letters of the lower-cased question string (digits, punctuation, white space separate tokens)
  content word  a token of at least two letters that is not in the stop list STOP = FUNCTION_WORDS | PICTURE_FRAME
  lookup rule   edit i is a candidate on a query  iff  the query has an image whose md5 equals the md5 of the edit's image
                AND the query's question and the edit's question share at least one content word.
                Released edit (top-1): the candidate with the most shared content words; a tie goes to the lowest edit index.
                No calibration, no threshold, no guard.  An edit whose question has no content word can never be released (counted).
  The list was written from general knowledge of English and of the visual-question frame, not from token statistics of the
  benchmark; it is not changed afterwards (sha1 of the sorted list: 08f4a4b05448; 226 words).

  FUNCTION_WORDS (162): the English stop-word list of NLTK, split at the apostrophe as the tokeniser splits it, plus

    wasn we were weren what when where which while who whom whose why will with won would wouldn y you your yours

  PICTURE_FRAME (64): words that name the picture or the act of depicting it, in all inflections (nearly every visual
  question carries one, so without them any two questions about a picture would share a content word)

C. USE (CPU, no model; a full pass over the sequences of a T = 100 log takes well under a second)

    R  = retr.reference("retr", L, dr.rows, alpha=0.05, seed=dr.seed) # two-block rule per attribute on that draw; seed = seed of the split
    Rl = retr.reference("lookup", L)                                  # no calibration

    ev  = R.evaluate(positions=("tf",))                               # {part: {which: decision}} over fx.gate.row_sets(L): the form of fx.gate.evaluate
    st  = fx.metrics.gate_stats(L, ev)                                # the gate-level numbers of every other gate (checked: test fx_compat)
    R.rec, R.fingerprint                                              # JSON record (alpha, n, eta and bound per attribute, capped edits, guarantee), 12 hex digits

  A DECISION is a dict of arrays aligned with the sequences, with every key of rescope.KeyCache.decide / fx.gate.decide:
    fired, top_edit (-1: none), n_cand, margin (>= 0 <=> fired), own, own_cand, own_top, near (always False: scores are exact), rows, which,
    resolves_edits; plus cand (n, T) bool, rank, and for retr n_img_pass / n_q_pass (edits passing ONE attribute).  which='steps' adds step_fired,
    step_top, step_near.  The decision of a sequence depends on its image content and question string only, NOT on the key: it is the same at
    'tf', 'prompt', 'decode' and in every forward of a decode, and the same for every calibration `which` of fx.draws (tf / all / ...).

D. DEFINITIONS
  score        image: cosine of the CLIP embeddings of the query's image and the edit's image; question: cosine of the MiniLM embeddings of the two
               question keys (strip, lower case).  Stored embeddings are unit-norm float32 on the 2^-24 grid, so the float64 dot product IS the
               cosine and is exact: scores are bit-for-bit functions of the stored files (no BLAS / batch / device dependence; test exact_scores).
               A query without an image has image score -inf for every edit: it never raises a baseline and nothing can be released on it.
  calibration  (task 4) the library's two-block rule (rescope.two_block_rule: exact rank, strict gate, floating-point-safe thresholds) applied to
               EACH attribute's [calibration queries x edits] score matrix with the SAME blocks (rescope.split_blocks(n, split, seed)):
                   b_a[i]  = largest score_a of edit i over block 1                         (baseline of edit i for attribute a)
                   eta_a   = k-th smallest over block 2 of max_i (score_a[i] - b_a[i]),  k = ceil((1 - alpha)(n2 + 1))      (common scale of a)
                   edit i PASSES attribute a  iff  score_a[i] - b_a[i] > eta_a
               release rule = the conjunction: edit i is a candidate iff it passes the image attribute AND the question attribute.
  top-1        the candidate with the largest (score_img - b_img) + (score_q - b_q), the sum of the two margins (R4); the library's convention:
               the margin r - b is used, which differs from the excess over the calibrated level by the constant eta_img + eta_q; an exact tie
               goes to the lowest edit index.
  guards       the own request of every edit must keep passing both attributes (the analogue of the own-prompt guards of every calibrated gate):
               where the literal rule would silence it on an attribute, that threshold is capped at (1 - 1e-9) x the own score (library rule);
               such an edit then passes that attribute only for (numerically) its own image / its own question string.  Counted per attribute:
               rec['n_edits_capped'], ['n_edits_capped_image'], ['n_edits_capped_question'], ['n_edits_capped_both'], ['capped_edits'].
               allow_silence=True: the literal rule.
  behaviour    the library's scoped replay of the released edit: module output W_0 k + V_edit at the last prompt position (V = the values of the
               scoped log handed to forced_gate / RequestGate: hand it the driver's base gate and the values are the same rebased values as for
               every other gate), the pre-edit model, bit for bit, everywhere else and on every sequence on which nothing is released.

  WHY THE BOUND OF THE CALIBRATION THEOREM HOLDS FOR THE CONJUNCTION
    The theorem (two-block rule, uncapped): the feature map of an attribute (query -> its T scores) does not depend on the calibration or test
    queries (the edits' requests and two fixed pretrained encoders determine it), the baselines depend on block 1 only, so the block-2 margins
    and the margin of a fresh benign query are exchangeable, and
        P(A_a) <= floor(alpha (n2 + 1)) / (n2 + 1) <= alpha,     A_a = {some edit passes attribute a on the fresh query},   a = image, question.
    retr releases an edit only if ONE edit passes BOTH attributes.  That event R satisfies  R  subset of  A_image intersect A_question, hence
        P(R) <= min(P(A_image), P(A_question)) <= min(bound_image, bound_question) <= alpha        (rec['bound_false_firing']).
    No union bound and no independence between the attributes is needed: the conjunction releases no more than either attribute alone
    (test properties checks on every sequence that a release implies a passing edit on each attribute; mechanics.txt prints the three rates).
    With caps: a capped threshold lies below the conformal level of ITS attribute only.  If no edit is capped on the image attribute, every
    release still needs the literal image event, so P(R) <= bound_image holds exactly, however many question thresholds are capped (and the
    same with the roles exchanged); if both attributes have capped edits but no edit is capped on both, P(R) <= bound_image + bound_question;
    for an edit capped on both the bound is void (it releases only on, numerically, its own request).  rec['guarantee'] states which case holds
    and rec['bound_false_firing_with_caps'] is the bound that holds.
    What the bound does NOT say: it is marginal over the benign pool (image and text queries together).  retr can release on image queries only,
    so its rate among image queries alone is bounded by bound / (share of image queries), not by alpha.  It is a statement about queries
    exchangeable with the calibration queries: near-scope negatives are outside it, as for every gate.

E. WIRING INTO THE DRIVER (fx is not changed by this layer; what follows was run against fx as it stood on 2026-11-11 08:30 UTC, its files
   last modified 07:55 UTC; fx is under review: if its interfaces move, rerun tests fx_compat and behaviour_fx)
  gate level      section C: R.evaluate() -> fx.metrics.gate_stats(L, ev).  Records: R.rec (calibration), Rl.rec (lookup).
  behaviour       a reference system is not a function of the module input, so it is not a ScopedLog with thresholds.  Two exact routes:
    (a) memo path (recommended; tests fx_compat and behaviour_fx):
          dec = R.decide(rows);  S0 = fx.methods.base_gate(L)[0]
          for e, pos in retr.release_plan(dec).items():
              M = fx.methods.wrap(L, retr.forced_gate(S0, e), f"retr@{e}", rec={...})      # M.memo is True: the driver's memo path
              out = ses.tf(M, [rows[i] for i in pos])                                      # same memo signature (the value V_e) as any scoped gate
          a sequence with dec['fired'] False is the pre-edit model: its row is the cached pre-edit outcome (as the driver does for a silent gate).
          The outcome fields of the rows are final; the FIRING FLAGS of such a row are those of the forced gate (fired, top_last = e, one
          candidate): take n_cand_edits / own_fired_last / own_released_last from the decision (dec['n_cand'], dec['own_cand'], dec['own_top']).
          Decodes (D2, free generation, wrong target): the same gate; it releases e in every full forward that holds the last prompt position.
    (b) library path (tests request_gate and behaviour_smoke): ONE gate object in the interface baselines.py documents (firing / respond / apply):
          g = R.gate(S_on_device, dec);  rows = retr.gate_rows(ev, g, items)               # g.select(it['sid']) before the forward(s) of each item
          The rows carry the decision's flags (fired, top_last, n_cand_edits, own_fired_last, own_released_last).  One forward per item, no memo.
          A RequestGate must be told the sequence (select): it cannot be passed to fx.behave / Evaluator.tf_rows over several items as it is.
  calibration     dr.rows of any fx.draws.calibration draw (REPAIR pool, benign pool, contaminated): all are sequences of the key cache.  Pass
                  seed=dr.seed: the blocks are then the blocks of every other gate of that draw.  retr.default_draw(log, 200, d) equals
                  fx.draws.calibration(L, 200, d).rows; the driver's draws of other sizes are nested and must be taken from fx.draws.
  logs            any log whose edits are edits of a cached stream family (Q: up to 2000 edits, QK: MMKE-Bench 500, L: 1000); the scores of a
                  longer log contain those of its prefixes.  NOT covered: the scout's caches (10_final/cache/QO one-hop family, blip2): they have
                  no attribute table and no embeddings here (retr.edits raises LookupError / FileNotFoundError).

H. THINGS TO KNOW
  * Capped edits (measured on the two development logs, draw 0; out/mechanics.txt).  The question baseline of an edit is the largest cosine
    between its question and the question of a block-1 calibration query; its median over the edits is 0.90 / 0.87, and 14 / 22 of the 200
    calibration queries ask an edit's question word for word.  With eta about 0.16 the literal question threshold (baseline + eta) exceeds 1
    for 59 / 58 of the 100 edits: those edits are capped and pass the question attribute only for their own question string.  No edit was
    capped on the image attribute, so the false-firing bound holds through it (section D).  A capped edit cannot be released on a text
    rephrase; whether that is the main limit of retr's text-rephrase coverage was not analysed further.
  * Image rephrases use another image of the same entity: the image cosine there (out/tests/rephrase_scores.json) is what limits retr on
    I-Gen; lookup cannot cover an image rephrase at all (another file, another md5) unless the benchmark repeats the image.
  * Questions repeat across edits (out/tests/facts.json): the question attribute alone does not identify an edit; the edit images of a stream
    are all distinct by md5.  A few edits have an image rephrase that IS their own image (same md5).
  * Reproducibility of the image embeddings.  On the GPU model of the build (RTX 5090; checked on both cards) a re-embedding is bit for bit.  On
    the CPU the float32 CLIP forward differs by up to 2.2e-6 per component (a cosine by up to 1.8e-6): test reembed_images_cpu does NOT meet
    its 1e-6 criterion and is listed as such in the test table.  The stored matrix (md5 in emb/meta.json) is the reference; a rebuild on another
    device or another GPU model is not guaranteed to reproduce it bit for bit, so never mix rows of two builds.  Scores themselves are exact
    functions of the stored files.
  * Last prompt position.  A reference system releases whatever the key is, so in a decode it also releases on the LLaVA sequences whose
    teacher-forced last prompt position is one token later than the decode's (lp_shift = 1), where the log's own detectors cannot fire.  The
    released value was written for the teacher-forced position; the outcome there is whatever the model does with it.
  * The vision tower COULD be loaded alone (391 tensors of one safetensors shard); the fallback to a backbone's own tower was not needed.
  * retr and lookup decide on the query's attributes, so they need the attribute table.  A query that is not a sequence of the key cache: embed
    its image / question with encoders.py (ImageEncoder.embed_paths, QuestionEncoder.embed on question.strip().lower(): same preprocessing and
    storage form), take the float64 dot products with st.EI[E.img] / st.EQ[E.q] (st = retr.store(cache), E = retr.edits(log)) and call retr.decide.
  * How the stop list of section A came about: it was written after reading the caches' documentation and about twenty example prompts of the
    cache index (inspection of the format), before any embedding, score or decision existed; no token statistics were computed, then or later,
    and the list was not touched after 06:27 UTC (stoplist.py; this file's section A: 06:50 UTC, byte-identical since).
  * Scale: retr.scores for the longest streams (T = 2000 x 11800 sequences; T = 1000 x 8800) takes 1.6 s / 0.2 s and 0.7 GB (timing only; no
    threshold or decision was computed on any log other than the two development logs).
